Prophets are regarded as individuals chosen by the divine to deliver messages, provide guidance, and convey teachings to humanity. Their roles and attributes can vary significantly across different religious traditions.
📖 Biblical Perspective
-  In Christianity, prophets are seen as God's messengers who convey His will to the people. For example, in Amos 3:7, it is stated, "Surely the Sovereign LORD does nothing without revealing his plan to his servants the prophets."
📜 Islamic Perspective
-  In Islam, prophets are known as "nabi" or "rasul" and are considered chosen individuals who are sent by Allah to guide humanity. The Quran emphasizes that prophets were sent to all communities, as stated in Quran 10:47, "And for every nation is a messenger."
✡️ Jewish Perspective
-  In Judaism, prophets are viewed as individuals called by God to speak on His behalf, often providing guidance and warnings. Deuteronomy 18:18 highlights this, where God says, "I will raise up for them a prophet like you from among their fellow Israelites, and I will put my words in his mouth."
🕉️ Hindu Perspective
-  Hinduism does not specifically focus on prophets in the same way as the Abrahamic faiths. However, divine guidance is often delivered through avatars or incarnations of deities, such as Krishna, who imparts wisdom like in Bhagavad Gita 4.7-8: "Whenever there is a decline in righteousness, and an increase in unrighteousness, O Arjuna, at that time I manifest Myself on earth."
☸️ Buddhist Perspective
-  Buddhism does not have prophets in the traditional sense. Instead, the Buddha is seen as an enlightened teacher who shares the path to enlightenment, with the Dhammapada 1.1 emphasizing the importance of understanding and mindfulness: "All that we are is the result of what we have thought."
In conclusion, prophets across these traditions are seen as intermediaries between the divine and humanity, delivering messages and guidance. While the form and function of these figures can differ, they commonly serve as conduits of spiritual truth and moral instruction.
